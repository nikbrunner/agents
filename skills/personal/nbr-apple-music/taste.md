# Nik's music taste

What a library scan cannot tell: lasting preferences from interviews and approved playlists. Read it in the Brief, before any search. Nik's own words in quotes.

## Across the board

- Open and wide-ranging. A playlist can draw on any lane below.
- Mix about half music from the library with half discovery. Library tracks anchor the list, and new finds sit around them.
- Never: cheap chart pop, K-pop, and "music made to get laid", meaning hipster music that sounds calculated.
- Never: thin, breathy, high male vocals that sound pretentious, such as Bon Iver or James Blake. The test is authenticity, not pitch. Thom Yorke and Matt Bellamy are fine.
- Never: Nils Frahm. "Completely avoid", so his collaborations stay out too.
- Never: showy soloing. "What I hate is this absolute masturbation on the saxophone", an instrument that runs through all the notes "like a super attention-seeking instrument". Piano can do it too. A melody played plainly is fine, on a saxophone as well. Bill Evans's Peace Piece failed as "piano masturbation": a calm piece whose right hand wanders ever further over a fixed bass is still showy. Stradal's piano transcription of Bach's BWV 528 Andante failed as "horrible", for its "attention seeking virtuosity". "Virtuosity does not impress me." Kumi Tanioka's Fuyukodachi went out of First Light for the same reason, a piano piece that keeps building toward display. Both judge the style of the piece, never the artist: Tanioka, Stradal and Bach stay open. Dissonance is welcome and never the problem: "I like dissonance", as in The Body from Annihilation (Ben Salisbury & Geoff Barrow), which he loves.

## Lanes

**Club: minimal and techno.** Dark, rolling, groovy and percussive. Hypnotic, but never loose or housey. Anchors: Gaiser, Troy Pierce, Plastikman, Marc Houle, Heartthrob, Robag Wruhme, Mathew Jonson, and SiS's Cécille records (Marcha Oscura is the reference track). The sweet spot is the M_nus, Cécille and Cocoon sound of 2005–2011, at about 125–130 BPM. No vocals in this lane. Two arcs he likes for a long set. One opens with dark, moody ambient, builds slowly, and closes the same way. The other starts groovy, stays groovy, and gets stranger toward the middle and end, with no ambient bookends. Gaiser and Troy Pierce carry that weirder last third. His own tracks are released as Shuqq on Redukt Digital. Use them sparingly, since he prefers other artists. The playlist "Hypnotic Techno" is off limits as a source.

**Harder electronic.** The Prodigy, Noisia, Carpenter Brut, Amon Tobin. There are many tracks in the library but few plays, so ask before leaning on this lane.

**Neoclassical and ambient.** A piece needs either a real melody or real texture. Texture alone is welcome: "just curated sounds, so to say, can really be beautiful". What fails is the generic soft piano piece with neither, "a lazy excuse for a soft piece of music": "hollow", "soft but without depth", it "does not evoke any feeling". Hania Rani passes: Glass (Esja), "I really like this one", and F Major (Home), "one of the most beautiful tracks ever". Max Richter is the most played artist in the library and passes with Recomposed, The Blue Notebooks and In a Landscape; Sleep and While The Flood Rages fail as "pretentious". Ólafur Arnalds is "a bit better" than Nils Frahm and still mostly fails. C418, Boards of Canada and Aphex Twin's Selected Ambient Works 85–92 stand.

**Soundtracks.** Vangelis and Zimmer/Wallfisch on Blade Runner, Joe Hisaishi, Rob Simonsen. "Love Salisbury": Ben Salisbury's scores, with Geoff Barrow and others (Annihilation, Ex Machina, Devs). Amaya Corporation from Devs is the reference for "deep ambient": "so beautiful", "the stuff that gets me going", with a pipe-like lead instrument he singles out. What makes it work is "a recognizable melody that goes through that ambient". Ambient that is "basically just a random synthesizer with long releases" has "no quality to it" and fails. Jóhann Jóhannsson's Arrival score is "absolutely awesome". This dark material belongs in the playlist "Void". "First Light" is the light morning playlist, "it should be light, not dark". For calm playlists he wants quiet scores that are "not obvious choices": James Newton Howard (The Village, Lady in the Water), Thomas Newman, Gustavo Santaolalla, Jóhann Jóhannsson, Nick Cave & Warren Ellis.

**First Light** runs from dreamy to awake. It opens electronic (Aphex Twin's Rhubarb, "it fits well"), turns acoustic with Grieg's Morning Mood as the sunrise, and peaks late with Spring 0 and Spring 1 from Max Richter's Recomposed, "it really feels like waking up", at the end of the awake stretch. A quiet electronic close follows and ends on Budd & Eno's First Light. Sound changes in long blocks, electronic then acoustic, never track by track. Inside a block, neighbours share or relate in key where sources give one. Aphex Twin's Selected Ambient Works Volume II (Rhubarb, Lichen), Avril 14th and Aisatsana stand alongside 85–92.

**Trip-hop and atmosphere.** Massive Attack, Portishead (Third is on vinyl), Hidden Orchestra, Ours Samplus, Khruangbin.

**Songs with voices.** Radiohead and Thom Yorke (OK Computer, In Rainbows, The Eraser), Björk, ROSALÍA, Billie Eilish, Norah Jones, Magdalena Bay, Muse, The Tallest Man on Earth. Whether a vocal works "really depends on the actual person singing". Jackson C. Frank (Milk and Honey) is "totally fine", Eddie Vedder and Agnes Obel are okay, and Bob Dylan is "nice, but can be annoying with his voice".

**Jazz and guitar.** GoGo Penguin, Keith Jarrett, Rodrigo y Gabriela, John Renbourn. "Smooth jazz" from him means quiet ballad and ECM-style jazz with strong melodies, never stock café jazz by anonymous ensembles. GoGo Penguin's Man Made Object is a favourite as a whole album, Protest above all. Its lane is acoustic trios that play like a techno machine: ostinato piano, breakbeat drums, repetition over soloing. The playlist "Man Made Object" holds it: Gondwana (Mammal Hands, Portico Quartet, Sunda Arc, Svaneborg Kardyb), Dawn of Midi, Nik Bärtsch's Ronin, Neil Cowley Trio, Brandt Brauer Frick.
